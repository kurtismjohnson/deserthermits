Corners

He'd be at the corner of Piedmont and 10th every Saturday morning. All he'd have with him was a bucket to sit on and his guitar, and if it was raining, he'd bring a giant umbrella. He couldn't sing well and his guitar would be out of tune, warped and cracked by the four seasons of the Deep South. But like clockwork, the crowds would gather. They'd listen as he used the same three chords to pry open their hearts and plant the seeds of absolution. "No enemies, no foes." Shoulder to shoulder they would spill into traffic, mimosas in hand. They'd raise their glasses and toast, but they'd be envious; envious that he had something to say, and envious that anyone would listen. Their bodies wrapped in his melodies, time would stand still, and it would remain that way until it was time to carry their fruits to the four corners of the earth.
