Corners

He'd be at the corner of Piedmont and 10th every Saturday morning. All he'd have with him was a bucket to sit on and his guitar, and if it was raining, he'd bring a giant umbrella. He couldn't sing well and his guitar would be out of tune, the spruce warped and cracked by the four seasons of the Deep South. But like clockwork, the crowds would gather. They'd listen as he'd use the same three chords to pry open their hearts and plant the seeds of absolution. "No enemies, no foes." Shoulder to shoulder they would spill into traffic, mimosas in hand. They'd raise their glasses and toast, as citrus and champagne would fall at their feet. Their bodies, wrapped in his melodies, would be caught in the deep wrinkles of his skin that had been warped and cracked by devotion. Time would stand still. Soon it would be time to carry their fruits to the four corners of the earth, but instead the fruit would be squeezed into their own empty glasses, consumed, bodies satiated. But next week would be different.
